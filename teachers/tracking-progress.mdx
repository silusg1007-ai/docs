ScienceLab's progress tracking tools help you stay on top of every student's engagement — from the first time they click play on a video to when they submit their final lab report. Whether you're checking in mid-assignment or reviewing results after a unit, the data you need is always a few clicks away.

## Class-Level Progress View

The class progress view gives you a bird's-eye picture of how your entire class is moving through an assignment. At the top of each assignment row, a **completion bar** shows the percentage of students who have submitted, updated in real time.

Each student's status is color-coded for quick scanning:

<CardGroup cols={4}>
  <Card title="Not Started" icon="circle">
    **Gray** — the student hasn't opened the assignment yet.
  </Card>
  <Card title="In Progress" icon="circle-half-stroke">
    **Yellow** — the student has opened the assignment but hasn't submitted.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Submitted" icon="circle-check">
    **Green** — the student has submitted and is awaiting your review.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Graded" icon="circle-dot">
    **Blue** — you've reviewed the submission and returned feedback.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Click the **Sort by Completion Rate** button at the top of the assignment list to reorder assignments from lowest to highest completion. This quickly surfaces the assignments where students are falling behind so you can intervene before the due date passes.

## Individual Student Progress

When you need to understand one student's experience in depth, the individual progress view gives you the full picture.

- Click any student's name in your roster to open their complete activity history.
- Review **exact timestamps** for when they started each piece of content, how long they spent actively working, and when they submitted.
- Read every quiz answer, data table entry, and written response the student submitted — no need to flip through paper packets.

This detail is especially useful during parent conferences, when supporting students with IEPs, or when a student claims they completed work you don't see reflected in their grade.

## Exporting Grade Data

ScienceLab's export tool lets you pull your class data into any gradebook or LMS you already use.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the assignment or class view">
    Navigate to the assignment you want to export from, or go to your full class view to export all assignments at once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Export">
    Click the **Export** button in the upper-right corner of the view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Download the CSV">
    A CSV file downloads to your device containing student names, scores, and completion dates for every included assignment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import into your gradebook">
    Open your gradebook tool and use its standard import function to load the file. ScienceLab's CSV format is compatible with **Google Classroom**, **Canvas**, **Schoology**, and most other standard gradebook tools.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  ScienceLab does not sync automatically with LMS gradebooks — you'll need to import the CSV manually each time you want to update your external gradebook with new scores.
</Info>

## Identifying Students Who Need Help

Catching a struggling student early makes a real difference. ScienceLab's **At Risk** filter helps you spot students who may be falling behind before it shows up in their grade.

- Click **At Risk** in the filter bar of any assignment view to surface students who haven't started an assignment within 2 days of the due date.
- Review the flagged students and, if appropriate, send a reminder message directly from the dashboard using the **Send Reminder** button next to each name.

<Tip>
  Use the At Risk filter the day before an assignment is due as part of your end-of-day routine. A quick reminder message from you is often all a student needs to log in and get started.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Reminder messages are sent through ScienceLab's internal messaging system and appear in the student's dashboard. They are not delivered via email or SMS, so students must log in to see them.
</Warning>
