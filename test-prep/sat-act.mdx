While the SAT and ACT don't test specific science facts, they test your ability to read graphs, interpret experimental data, and evaluate scientific arguments — exactly the skills ScienceLab virtual labs develop.

## Skills the Tests Measure

The SAT and ACT Science sections reward students who can work quickly and accurately with scientific information — not students who have memorized the most content. Here's what you'll need to do well:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Graph and Table Interpretation" icon="chart-bar">
    Read and extract information from graphs, tables, and figures — including identifying trends, outliers, and relationships between variables.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Multi-Experiment Comparison" icon="flask">
    Compare results from multiple experiments and identify how changing one variable affects the outcome across different trials.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Hypothesis and Conclusion Evaluation" icon="magnifying-glass">
    Evaluate whether a hypothesis is supported by the data and whether a conclusion is logically drawn from the evidence presented.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Experimental Design" icon="clipboard">
    Understand how experiments are set up — including identifying independent and dependent variables, controls, and sources of experimental error.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Recommended Practice

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete 2–3 virtual labs per week">
    Consistency matters more than volume. Aim for 2–3 labs per week, and focus your energy on the analysis questions rather than rushing through the lab setup.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read data before answering">
    Before you answer any analysis question, spend a moment reading all the data in your table and graph. Identify the trend, the range, and any outliers — this habit pays off on timed tests.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use post-lab questions as timed practice">
    Work through the post-lab questions as a timed exercise. Aim for 2–3 minutes per question to match the pacing the ACT Science section demands.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  The ACT Science section is more about reading comprehension of scientific passages than memorized facts — ScienceLab's analysis questions are great practice for this format. If you can explain what your lab data shows in writing, you're already doing what the test asks.
</Tip>
