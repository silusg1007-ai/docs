ScienceLab's physics virtual labs give students hands-on experience with the principles of motion, energy, waves, and electricity through interactive simulations that respond in real time to student inputs.

## Lab List by Grade Band

Physics labs span from foundational force-and-motion concepts for early learners to quantitative kinematics and circuit analysis for high school students. All labs are NGSS-aligned and browser-based.

| Grade Band | Available Labs |
|------------|---------------|
| **K–2** | Pushes and Pulls Explorer, Light and Shadow Simulation |
| **3–5** | Simple Machines Lab (lever, pulley, inclined plane), Energy Transformation Observer |
| **6–8** | Newton's Laws Simulator, Projectile Motion Modeler, Wave Properties Lab |
| **9–12** | Advanced Projectile Motion (2D kinematics), Electrical Circuit Builder, Electromagnetic Induction Lab, Optics Ray Diagram Lab |

## Featured Lab — Projectile Motion Modeler

**Grades 6–8 and 9–12 | ~35 minutes**

The Projectile Motion Modeler lets you explore how launch angle and initial velocity affect the path of a moving object — with real-time trajectory visualization.

- Launch a virtual projectile and adjust the initial velocity and launch angle before each trial
- Observe and record peak height, horizontal range, and total time of flight for each combination you test
- Compare results across multiple trials to identify patterns — including why maximum range occurs at a 45° launch angle
- The **9–12 version** extends the lab with vector component calculations, requiring you to resolve initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components

## Featured Lab — Electrical Circuit Builder

**Grades 9–12 | ~40 minutes**

The Electrical Circuit Builder lets you design and test both series and parallel circuits using drag-and-drop components — no wiring, no short circuits, no blown fuses.

- Drag and drop components — batteries, resistors, light bulbs, and switches — onto a virtual breadboard to build your circuit
- Attach a virtual voltmeter and ammeter to measure voltage and current at any point in the circuit
- Calculate theoretical resistance using Ohm's Law and compare your calculated values to the simulation's measured readings

<Tip>
  In the Circuit Builder lab, always connect the voltmeter in parallel and the ammeter in series — the simulation will flag incorrect connections just like a real circuit would, giving you immediate feedback before you record any data.
</Tip>
