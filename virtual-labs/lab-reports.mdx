Every ScienceLab virtual lab produces a lab report — a structured document that captures your hypothesis, procedure observations, data, analysis, and conclusion. Writing strong lab reports is a core science practice skill, and ScienceLab's built-in CER templates make the process clear and structured.

## What's in a ScienceLab Lab Report

Each lab report is assembled automatically from the work you complete as you move through the lab tabs. Here's what every report includes:

- **Title and Date**: auto-filled with the lab name and your completion date
- **Hypothesis**: your prediction, entered before the lab begins
- **Data Table**: all measurements and observations recorded during the procedure
- **Graphs**: any charts generated from your data — either auto-generated by the simulation or created by you using the built-in graphing tool
- **Analysis Answers**: your written responses to the analysis questions on the Analysis tab
- **CER Conclusion**: Claim, Evidence, Reasoning — the structured conclusion you write on the Conclusion tab

## Writing a Strong CER Conclusion

The CER (Claim-Evidence-Reasoning) framework is the standard conclusion format used across all ScienceLab labs. A well-written CER has three distinct parts:

- **Claim**: a one-sentence answer to the lab's guiding question. Keep it direct and specific. For example: *"Increasing the concentration of the acid decreased the reaction time."*
- **Evidence**: 2–3 specific data points from your data table that support your claim. Always include numbers and units — vague statements like "the numbers went up" are not evidence.
- **Reasoning**: explain *why* your evidence supports your claim using scientific vocabulary from the unit. This is where you connect your data to the underlying concept.

<Accordion title="What if my data doesn't support my hypothesis?">
  Unexpected results are completely valid in science — in fact, they're some of the most interesting outcomes. If your data doesn't match your hypothesis, don't change your data to fit your prediction. Instead, describe what actually happened in your Claim, use your real data as Evidence, and in your Reasoning section suggest possible sources of error (measurement variability, simulation limitations, or a gap in your initial understanding of the concept). Your teacher is looking for honest scientific thinking, not a "correct" result.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I edit my lab report after submitting?">
  No — once you click Submit, your lab report is locked and sent to your teacher. If you made a mistake or submitted too early, contact your teacher directly. They can reset the lab so you can resubmit, though this is at their discretion. To avoid this situation, use the Download PDF option to review your full report before hitting Submit.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I download my lab report?">
  Click the **Download PDF** button on the Conclusion tab before you submit. You can also download a completed report after submission by going to **My Labs** on your dashboard, finding the lab in your history, and clicking **Download Report**. The PDF includes all sections of your report formatted for printing.
</Accordion>

## Downloading and Printing

You can save a copy of your lab report at any time — before or after submitting.

- Click **Download PDF** on the Conclusion tab to save a copy before submitting
- The PDF includes all sections of the report — hypothesis, data, analysis, and CER conclusion — formatted for printing
- Reports use a standard **8.5" × 11"** format and run **2–4 pages** depending on the length of the lab
