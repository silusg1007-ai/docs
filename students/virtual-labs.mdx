ScienceLab's virtual labs let you perform real science experiments entirely in your browser — no equipment, chemicals, or lab space required. Each lab follows the same structure as a real laboratory investigation: background reading, a hypothesis, a step-by-step procedure, data collection, and a written conclusion.

## Starting a Lab

You can launch a lab directly from your dashboard or by browsing the full catalog.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the Lab">
    Go to your **Dashboard** and open a lab from your Assigned section, or visit the **Lab Catalog** to browse all available labs by subject and grade band.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Start Lab">
    On the lab overview page, click the **Start Lab** button. If you've already begun the lab, this button will read **Continue Lab** instead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the Background and Hypothesis Sections">
    Before touching any equipment, read the **Background** tab carefully. It explains the science concept you're investigating. Then review the **Hypothesis** section and, if prompted, write your own prediction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Proceed to the Procedure Tab">
    Once you've reviewed the background, navigate to the **Procedure** tab to begin the experiment.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## During the Lab

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Follow Step-by-Step Instructions" icon="list-check">
    Each procedure step is presented one at a time. Read each instruction fully before interacting with the simulation — rushing through steps can lead to inaccurate results.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Record Data as You Go" icon="table">
    Enter your observations and measurements directly into the provided data tables. These are saved automatically and will be included in your submitted lab report.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use Simulated Instruments" icon="gauge">
    Some labs include interactive instruments — titration burettes, digital voltmeters, thermometers, and more. Click or drag as instructed to take readings, then record them in your data table.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Re-run Steps If Needed" icon="rotate">
    Made a mistake? Use the **Redo Step** button to repeat an individual procedure step without starting the entire lab over.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  Your progress is saved automatically after every step. You can close the browser tab at any time and return to your in-progress lab later — just click **Continue Lab** from your dashboard.
</Note>

## Submitting Your Lab

Once you've completed the procedure, follow these steps to finish and submit your report.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete All Procedure Steps">
    Make sure every step in the **Procedure** tab is marked complete. Incomplete steps are highlighted in yellow — click each one to finish before moving on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer the Analysis Questions">
    Navigate to the **Analysis** tab and answer all questions using data from your experiment. Reference specific values from your data table to support your answers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write Your Conclusion">
    Open the **Conclusion** tab. If your teacher enabled the **CER template**, structure your response as a Claim, followed by Evidence from your data, followed by Reasoning that connects the two. Otherwise, follow your teacher's written instructions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Submit">
    When all tabs are complete, click the **Submit** button. Your finished lab report is sent directly to your teacher for review. You'll see a confirmation message once the submission is received.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Once you click **Submit**, you cannot edit your lab report unless your teacher sends it back to you for revision. Review all your answers before submitting.
</Warning>

## Tips for Success

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Read the Background before you start">
    The Background section isn't just introductory filler — it contains the concepts and vocabulary you'll need to complete the Analysis Questions and Conclusion. Students who skip it often struggle to explain their results.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Fill in the data table every time">
    Even if a result seems obvious, record it in the data table. Analysis Questions frequently ask you to reference specific data points, and a complete table makes writing your conclusion much easier.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Use the vocabulary list for unfamiliar terms">
    Every lab has a linked vocabulary list. Open it in a separate browser tab while you work so you can quickly look up any terms you don't recognize without losing your place in the procedure.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Watch the paired concept video first">
    If a concept video is linked on the lab page, watch it before you begin. Labs go faster and results make more sense when you already understand the underlying science.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
